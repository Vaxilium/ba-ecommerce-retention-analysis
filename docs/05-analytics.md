## 1. Repeat-purchase driver model (logistic regression)

**Target**: repeat purchase within 90 days (`is_repeat_90d`). **Population**: 15,691 customers
with a fully observed 90-day window (no censoring). 75/25 stratified train/test split.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Test AUC | **0.636** (train 0.639) |
| Base repeat rate | 30.8% |
| Train / test rows | 11,768 / 3,923 |

Test AUC of 0.636 means the model separates repeaters from one-timers clearly better than
chance, but most of the signal is in who the customer is at acquisition (channel, discount) — behavioural
features at the first order (delivery, support) add real but smaller signal. For a *prioritisation* model
this is enough: the top predicted decile repeats at **1.52×** the base rate
(47% vs 31%), the bottom decile at
**0.55×**.

### Limitations

- Synthetic data with known structure; real-world effect sizes will differ.
- Logistic regression is linear in log-odds — interactions (e.g. paid social × heavy discount) are not
  explicit; observed effects are conditional associations, **not proof of causality**.
- >72h support tickets are rare (1 feature,
  wide CI) — direction matches operations data, magnitude uncertain.
- The model uses first-order + acquisition features only: no browsing, no email engagement, no seasonality
  beyond month sinusoids.

**Recommended action per segment**

- **champions** (21.3% of customers, 70.7% of revenue): Reward & referral: early access, loyalty perks, ask for reviews.
- **needs_attention** (10.7% of customers, 10.3% of revenue): Reactivate gently: win-back email with best-seller picks.
- **at_risk** (7.9% of customers, 5.6% of revenue): Priority win-back: high-value lapsed — personal outreach / stronger offer.
- **new** (14.2% of customers, 3.5% of revenue): Onboard: welcome series, first-repeat incentive within 30 days.
- **hibernating** (10.6% of customers, 3.3% of revenue): Low-cost reactivation only: newsletter + seasonal campaigns.
- **lost** (21.4% of customers, 2.7% of revenue): Do not spend: suppress from paid audiences, keep email only.
- **loyal** (2.0% of customers, 1.6% of revenue): Upsell & cross-sell: bundles, category expansion, VIP tier invite.
- **potential_loyalist** (2.4% of customers, 1.3% of revenue): Nurture: personalised recommendations, second-purchase nudge.
- **about_to_sleep** (9.3% of customers, 1.1% of revenue): Win-back candidate: time-limited offer, low-cost channel first.

Read with the KPI tree: champions alone carry 71% of revenue
— protecting them (service quality, delivery) matters more than winning back small one-timers.
The prime win-back pool (4,940 customers inactive 120–540 days) mixes
`at_risk` (higher past value) and `hibernating`/`about_to_sleep` (lower value) — the A/B test below prices the play.

## 3. Simulated A/B test: win-back email

**Design**: the eligible pool is the 5,057 opted-in customers whose last order was
120-540 days ago. Randomized 50/50 (seed 42).
Outcome: any purchase within **60 days** (the standard win-back measurement window).

**Control rate is not assumed**: it is anchored to the *empirical natural reactivation rate* measured from
order history — 5.8% of 180-day-inactivity spells end with a purchase
within 60 days (8,072 spells observed).
The email's effect (35% relative lift) is the scenario input being tested.

### Results

| Arm | Customers | Conversions | Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Control (no email) | 2,528 | 128 | 5.06% |
| Treatment (win-back email) | 2,529 | 191 | 7.55% |

- Absolute lift: **+2.49 pp** | Relative lift: **+49.2%**
- Two-proportion z-test: **z = 3.64, p = 0.00027** → significant at α = 0.05
- 95% CI (absolute): [+1.15 pp, +3.83 pp] |
  95% CI (relative): [+22.7%, +75.6%]

### Power analysis

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| MDE (80% power, α=5%, n=5,057) | **1.84 pp** (32% relative) |
| Sample size per arm to detect a 25% relative lift | 4,095 |

The achieved sample detects lifts ≥ 32% relative; the observed lift
(49%) is comfortably above it.

### Economics and decision

- Average margin per order (these customers): **$40.34**
- Incremental conversions (observed lift × treatment arm): **63**
- Incremental margin: **$2,539.63** vs program cost **$126.45**
  ($0.05/email) → **ROI 19.1×**
- Conservative case (CI lower bound of lift): incremental margin **$1,174.19**
  — still above cost.

> **Decision: ROLL OUT.** The win-back email is significant on the primary metric, positive on the
> conservative CI bound, and its unit economics work even at the low end. Full 3-scenario program case:
> `docs/06-business-case.md`.

## 4. Honest limitations (all sections)

- Data is synthetic; patterns were intentionally engineered and then *rediscovered* — the value shown here
  is the analysis method, not the specific effect sizes.
- A/B results are simulated with a seeded process; the statistics (z-test, CI, MDE) are exactly as they
  would be computed on real experiment data, but they validate a **pipeline**, not a real campaign.
- Cohort comparisons (docs/04) are observational: mix effects and within-group effects are entangled
  (mitigated, not solved, by the decomposition).
